Collection Tiles

The round tiles for the listening collections (Focus, Night Drive, Nightcap and the rest) are a different format from a class cover and take a different prompt. A tile is a moment seen through the listener’s eyes, not an abstract idea. It carries no text, and it is shown at icon size, cropped to a circle. Locked 2026-11-08, after three rounds of tests.

Rules

  • Color: each tile takes the dominant field color of its energy group; the five colors are on Art & Color.
  • Viewpoint: first-person, from inside the moment: sunk in the armchair, at the stove, behind the wheel. Nothing of the viewer’s own body is visible, hands included.
  • A scene, not an icon: photographic framing with real depth, a near foreground and a background behind it, filling the whole square.
  • Contrast: the main subject is solid, crisp-edged, and clearly lighter or darker than everything around it, so it reads at thumbnail size.
  • Low detail: three to five elements in all; the background is two or three big plain shapes.
  • No circles: no circular glows, light pools, halos, bokeh, sun discs or motion rings. Transparency is allowed only for straight or slanted beams, steam, haze and sheer fabric, and never over the subject.
  • Circle-safe: everything that matters sits well inside the central circle; the corners hold only plain background that can be cut away.
  • Variety: vary the viewpoint across the set (down a table, up at a ceiling, across a fire, through a windshield, down a corridor). Do not repeat “object left, window right,” and do not reuse another tile’s main object.
  • People: none. The exception is a crowd that is the whole point (Cocktails, Dance Floor, House Party), and then only faceless, flat silhouettes in the background.
  • No text: no words, letters, numbers, logos, brand names, signs, dial markings or screen text anywhere.
  • Full bleed: flat to all four square edges with sharp square corners; no frayed, worn or rounded edges (the app does the rounding).

What Did Not Work

  • Too much detail. The first round read well large and turned to mush at tile size. Fixed with the Contrast and Low detail rules.
  • Flat views from straight above. The second round answered the detail problem by turning every tile into a flat icon (a plate seen from above, a notebook from above). That lost the sense of being somewhere. Keep the scene; cut the clutter.
  • Big circles behind the subject. A sun disc behind a kettle, a light pool over a notebook and a candle glow over a plate each hid the thing they framed. Bokeh dots turned a martini into a tree. Hence the No circles rule.
  • The same layout again and again. An object on the left and a window on the right became the default. Hence the Variety rule.

Examples

Prompt Format

Draw this

NAME (no text)
- Viewpoint: first-person, [where the listener is and what they are looking at]; nothing of the viewer's own body is visible.
- Scene: [the subject, close; one or two supporting elements; a plain background]
- Contrast: [the subject] is fully opaque, crisp-edged, and clearly [lighter / darker] than [the background]; the subject must read instantly at thumbnail size.
- Light: no circular glows, halos, light pools, or bokeh; the only transparency is [one slanted beam / the steam / the haze], which never covers the subject.
- Circle-safe: the image will be cropped to a circle; keep [the subject and supports] well inside the central circle, with only [plain background] in the corners.
- Colors: [group color name] (#hex) as the dominant field; [each element by name and hex]
- Style: mid-century screen-print poster; flat, simplified forms; [mood in a few words]; photographic framing with real depth.
- Detail: low detail; no more than [N] elements; no shading, highlights, or gradients; no [exclusions], hands, faces, or people.
- No text: no words, letters, numbers, logos, or writing anywhere.
- Full bleed: runs flat to all four square edges; sharp square corners; no frame, border, or worn edges.
- Finish: aged screen print; subtle paper grain; ink slightly faded but colors rich.

A Worked Example

Nightcap, in the Quiet group. A late hour on your own: whisky in front of the fire, seen from low in the chair.

Draw this

NIGHTCAP (no text)
- Viewpoint: first-person, sitting low in a chair in front of a fireplace late at night, eye level with the hearth; nothing of the viewer's own body is visible.
- Scene: one heavy whisky tumbler with amber whisky and one ice cube, close in the lower foreground on a small side table; behind it, filling the middle of the frame, a stone fireplace with bright, simple flames.
- Contrast: the tumbler and whisky are fully opaque, crisp-edged, and read as a clear dark-and-amber silhouette against the bright fire; the room around the fireplace is very dark.
- Light: no circular glows, halos, or bokeh; the only transparency is one slanted, semi-transparent wedge of firelight across the floor.
- [Circle-safe, Colors, Style, Detail, No text, Full bleed and Finish lines as in the format above]

Production

Send one prompt at a time to 🖼️ Make Covers and wait for the image before sending the next. Save each tile as a 320×320 JPEG under moods/, named for the collection’s key in MODES in src/data.js (nightcap.jpg, road.jpg). A new collection needs its MODES entry before its tile shows up. Check the tile at its real size in the Collections page before calling it done: if the subject is not obvious at a glance, the prompt needs less in it, not more.
